英文评委问答

Why did you flag 19 more deaths with the same 521 alerts?

Adding measured lactate changed who ranked in the top 521. On the same scored admissions, the model with lactate flagged 149 of 318 deaths, compared with 130 using organ function and vital signs alone. That’s a net gain of 19, or six percentage points.

Why did 169 deaths still go unflagged?

Those patients ranked below the cutoff for 521 alerts. At that workload, the model flagged about 47 percent of deaths among scored admissions. We’d review the missed cases to understand what the model could and couldn’t see at 24 hours.

Were 1,402 additional reviews worth it?

The second tier flagged 97 more deaths, at roughly 14 to 15 extra reviews per additional death flagged. It put 97.8 percent of eligible admissions on an alert or review list and still missed five deaths. I’d use that as a benchmark and test a smaller queue before routine use.

How would you validate the model externally?

We’d test the frozen model in an independent ICU cohort using the same 24-hour assessment and seven-day outcome. We’d agree on the alert budget in advance, compare the models with and without lactate, and report scoring coverage, calibration, missed deaths, and review workload.
